The Premium Ladder

How many times the median full-time salary does a career in banking, consulting or Big Tech pay? At the first job, about 1.5 to 3 times, almost everywhere. The gap between countries opens later.

What the numbers say

1.4x to 2.9x

The first job is not where the gap is.

In the first year, banking pays 1.7x (Switzerland) to 2.9x (the United States and France) the median full-time salary, and consulting 1.4x to 2.1x. A New York analyst earns about 2.9x the median, not 5 to 10 times. An analyst in Milan earns 1.8x.

3.9x vs 12.7x

The countries split apart as careers lengthen.

At entry, the highest banking multiple is 1.7 times the lowest. Ten years in, the gap is 3.3 times: a director in New York earns 12.7x the median, one in Zurich 3.9x and one in Frankfurt 4.1x. Switzerland and Germany stay lowest: their median salaries are high, and EU rules cap German bank bonuses. France's estimate is higher still, but it is a ballpark built on a 2021 survey.

-27%

Tax narrows the premium in New York as much as in Europe.

Progressive tax cuts the multiple wherever it is large. A New York banker ten years in drops from 12.7x to 9.2x after federal, state and city tax, a 27% cut; London goes from 11.5x to 7.9x. Germany barely moves (4.1x to 3.6x): social contributions are capped, so the median worker gives up a similar share of pay as the banker.

3.3x vs 1.9x

Big Tech pays a bigger entry premium than banking in most of Europe.

A new-grad engineer at Google, Meta or Amazon starts at a higher multiple than a first-year banker in 5 of 7 countries. In Madrid it is 3.3x against 1.9x; in Zurich 3.1x against 1.7x. In New York the two are close (3.1x and 2.9x). The entry premium looks like a global-firm premium more than a finance one.

Read the high end with care.

Beyond ten years most figures are thin or ballpark, and several countries have no credible data at all. Years 15 and 20 describe the minority who make managing director or partner, and they leave out carry and deferred pay. Even at the top of the ladder, the multiples are in the tens. The fortunes in The Half Line are worth thousands of years of a median salary: pay alone does not build that gap.

How it is built

The multiple is total pay for the role divided by the national median gross salary of full-time workers, both in the same currency and the same year (2025). Because it is a ratio, no exchange rate or purchasing-power adjustment is needed.

Total pay is base salary plus bonus. Big Tech pay includes stock at grant value. Private-equity carry and partnership distributions beyond reported profit are not included.

The median comes from national statistics: BLS (US, 2025), ONS (UK, April 2025), and the EU Structure of Earnings Survey 2022 for the euro countries and Switzerland, raised to 2025 with each country's wage index. Same age compares roles at 0 to 6 years with workers under 30, and roles at 10 to 20 years with workers aged 30 to 49.

Each stage maps to a title: analyst, associate, VP, director, managing director in banking; entry hire, consultant, engagement manager, principal, partner in consulting; L3 to L8 (or the Meta and Amazon equivalents) in Big Tech. Banking and consulting entrants join after a bachelor's in the US and usually after a master's in continental Europe.

Quality. Every point is tagged. Sourced: two or more independent sources, or 20+ verified Levels.fyi data points. Thin: one source, a source from 2021 or 2022, or 5 to 19 data points. Ballpark: a source's own rough estimate, or a gap filled one rung up the ladder.

How gaps are filled. A missing stage is estimated only when the same country has real data at the stage just below. The estimate takes London's pay at that stage (or New York's, if London has no data) and scales it by the country's pay relative to London at the stage below. Estimates are never built on other estimates. Where nothing qualifies, the row says "No credible data".

Year 20 repeats year 15 for most countries, flagged as ballpark: no source separates managing-director, partner or principal-engineer pay by tenure.

Coverage. Seven countries. The Netherlands was dropped for lack of credible banking and consulting data. Private equity was left out because carry cannot be observed and its entry level does not match the other careers.

After-tax assumptions

  • One person, no children, living in the city of the job, 2025 tax rules, standard deductions only, no church tax.
  • The same rules on both sides. The median worker is taxed as if living in the same city, so the after-tax multiple compares like with like.
  • Employee social contributions are deducted at statutory rates and ceilings. Employer contributions sit outside gross pay on both sides.
  • Bonuses are taxed as ordinary income. Stock is taxed at grant value, as if vested in the same year.
  • Expat regimes are not applied: Italy's impatriati regime, Spain's Beckham law, France's impatriate regime. They can cut tax sharply for exactly these hires.
  • United States: federal income tax (2025 standard deduction $15,750), Social Security and Medicare, New York State and New York City income tax, paid family leave and disability levies. New York's high-income recapture is simplified.
  • United Kingdom: England, 2025/26 income tax and Class 1 National Insurance, with the personal allowance tapered above £100,000.
  • Switzerland: City of Zurich, computed with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration calculator (federal, cantonal, municipal tax and social contributions, including mandatory pension).
  • Germany: tax class I, statutory health and care insurance at average rates, childless surcharge, solidarity surcharge. Higher earners often switch to private health insurance, which is not modelled.
  • France: Paris, cadre contributions, the income-tax scale for 2025 income, the 10% professional deduction and the high-income contribution.
  • Italy: Milan, INPS contributions, IRPEF with 2025 employment credits and tax-wedge relief, Lombardy and Milan surcharges.
  • Spain: Madrid, Social Security up to the 2025 ceiling, state and Madrid income-tax scales.

Caveats

What the chart cannot see

  • It shows the ladder, not the odds. Each stage is what someone still on the track earns. Most entrants leave before VP or manager; few become managing director or partner. Years 15 and 20 describe survivors, not what a new analyst should expect.
  • Pay data thins out at the top. Entry pay is well documented. Above VP or manager, figures are mostly modelled or self-reported, and carry, partnership distributions and deferred stock are partly invisible.
  • The best local graduates may work abroad. Many strong Italian, Spanish and French graduates take London or New York offers. A low domestic multiple can reflect that the best-paid jobs sit elsewhere.
  • Hours differ. Banking and consulting often run 60 to 80 hours a week against about 40 for the median worker. Per hour, the premium is much smaller.
  • Taxes buy different things. After-tax pay ignores public healthcare and education, which differ widely between the US and Europe.

Data quality

  • France's banking figures are old. They come from a 2021 survey, a record bonus year, and 2022 ranges. They may overstate current pay.
  • Italy and Spain use market-wide sources. Michael Page (Italy) and JobRise (Spain) cover the M&A market broadly, not only the top banks, so they probably understate elite pay. Italian bonus percentages are ceilings.
  • Levels.fyi is self-reported and pools several years. Big Tech figures average Google, Meta and Amazon, with stock at grant value.
  • Banking bonuses swing by about 30% from year to year. Figures target the 2025 bonus cycle where the sources allow.

Comparability

  • EU and Swiss medians exclude firms with fewer than 10 employees, which pay less. This raises those medians and lowers those multiples by an estimated 5 to 10% relative to the US and UK.
  • Uprating is approximate. EU 2022 medians are raised to 2025 with the wages and salaries labour cost index (per hour, not per worker); Switzerland with the federal wage index.
  • Same-age medians are approximate for Europe. Eurostat publishes means, not medians, by age, so the median is scaled by the mean's age profile.
  • Entry ages differ. US entrants are about 22, continental Europeans about 23 to 24 after a master's.

Regulation and tax

  • The EU caps bank bonuses at 100% of fixed pay, or 200% with shareholder approval. The UK removed its cap in 2023. This shapes senior pay in Paris, Frankfurt, Milan and Madrid against London, New York and Zurich.
  • Expat tax regimes are excluded, so after-tax pay for foreign hires in Italy, Spain and France can be much higher than shown.
  • Employer costs differ. Gross pay excludes employer contributions (about 45% on top in France). Both sides of the ratio exclude them, so the multiple is consistent, but the cost to the employer is not.
