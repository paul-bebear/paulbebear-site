When beliefs, tastes and habits set, why it is not one age, and how each generation answers the last.

No single age. Several windows.

"The brain finishes at 25" has no single study behind it. Each thing locks on its own clock: nicotine mostly before 18, party loyalty around 18, personality ranks around 25, music taste in the early 30s. The age 18 to 25 matters most for how you think the economy and society work.

The cycle, as a hypothesis

Each generation's politics is set by what it watched work or fail between about 14 and 24. When one side's era ends badly, the young absorb the alternative, and the swing shows up in the electorate a generation later.

Stated here as a claim to test. Evidence on both sides below.

How much each age counts

Ghitza, Gelman and Auerbach fit 50 years of US presidential voting to a running tally of presidential approval, weighted by the voter's age at the time. Events barely register in early childhood, matter most from 14 to 24 with a peak near 18, and fade to near zero by 60.

Shape drawn from the paper's description (peak near 18, about three times the weight of later life, zero by about 60). Not the fitted values.

Evidence for

  • The model fits. Approval plus age weights explains 81% of the variation in US presidential voting trends over half a century.
  • Cohorts stay apart. White voters born in 1952 are consistently 5 to 10 points more likely to vote Democratic than those born in 1968.
  • The 1972 gap. White first-time voters under 25 backed Nixon at 53%, against 70% for those 25 and over. The largest age gap in the data.
  • Attitudes set young. Krosnick and Alwin found susceptibility to change is high in late adolescence and early adulthood, then drops.
  • The public is a thermostat. Across democracies, preferences move against the direction of policy: more spending makes people want less.
  • Outside the US. UK cohorts that came of age in Conservative-dominated decades lean more Conservative (Tilley, 2002).

Evidence against, or limits

  • Success, not only failure. The paper's own conclusion: generations form over long stretches of well-rated presidents, not a single defining failure.
  • Adults change too. The white South's switch to Republicans came from cohort replacement and from every cohort shifting at similar rates.
  • Labels move, policies less. Young Reagan-era cohorts shifted in party and self-described ideology, but not on most specific policy opinions.
  • Weaker outside white voters. The generational effect is over twice as large for non-Hispanic whites as for minorities as a whole.
  • Race, region and class split harder. Generation is one divide among several, and often not the biggest.
  • Hard to identify. Age, period and cohort are collinear, so any cohort model leans on assumptions. US data only.

The market you meet at 18 stays with you

Two findings, same mechanism: what you live through early gets more weight than what you read about.

Real stock returns during each cohort's 18 to 25 window

Risk appetite. Households that lived through low stock returns report less willingness to take financial risk, are less likely to own stocks, hold a smaller share in them, and expect lower returns. Recent experience weighs most, especially for the young, but early experience still shows decades later.

Malmendier and Nagel, QJE 2011. US Survey of Consumer Finances, 1960 to 2007.

Beliefs about how society works. People who live through a recession between 18 and 25 come to believe success depends more on luck than effort, support more redistribution, and trust public institutions less. The effect is permanent. Shocks still register up to about 40, rarely after.

Giuliano and Spilimbergo, Review of Economic Studies 2014 (NBER w15321).

What to watch. Your instincts about markets and the state are a sample of one regime. Before you act on them, check them against the long series above.

Your rank settles. Your level keeps moving.

Rank means your position among people your age on a trait, not social status. If you are more anxious than 70% of your peers at 20, you will probably still be around there at 40, even while everyone calms down together.

How stable your rank is, by age

Test-retest correlation over a 6.7-year gap, from 152 longitudinal studies (Roberts and DelVecchio, 2000). A 2022 update with 178,503 people found little further rise after 25 (Bleidorn et al.).

Three facts that make "locked in" too strong

The whole pack moves. On average people gain social dominance, conscientiousness and emotional stability, most of it between 20 and 40.

A correlation of .64 is loose. It explains about 40% of the variance in your later score. Plenty of individuals move.

Change can be engineered. Across 207 studies, interventions shifted trait scores by d = .37 in about 24 weeks, and the change held. People who set and completed behaviour goals moved toward the trait they wanted.

Roles drive it. Starting a first job makes people more conscientious than peers who have not started one. Experience also amplifies the traits you already have, so the environment you pick sets the direction.

Genes

About 40% of differences in personality trace to genes: .47 in twin studies, .22 in family and adoption studies.

The other 60% is mostly not your family home. Adopted siblings raised together end up with personality correlations near zero. What counts is the environment you do not share: friends, work, choices, luck.

Genes are a large part of why rank holds. Experience is most of why it moves.

Onset age is the lever

For substances, the windows close well before 25. The research mostly measures when people start and what that predicts. The status line on each uses the birth year above.

Nicotine

99%
of adults who smoke daily tried it by 26

Nearly 9 in 10 first tried by 18. In one study of 1,382 smokers, those who started before 20 were more dependent and less likely to quit than those who started later.

CDC; age-of-initiation study reported by The Sentinel.

Alcohol

40% vs 10%
later dependence: first drink before 15 vs at 21 or 22

About a fourfold gap in a US survey of 27,616 drinkers. Part of it is confounding: a 2008 follow-up that controlled for more risk factors still found about 50% higher odds of adult-onset dependence for a first drink before 15 than at 18 or later.

Most heavy drinkers cut down in their twenties. Leaving home tends to raise heavy drinking. Marriage, parenthood and work bring it down, most strongly for the heaviest drinkers.

Grant and Dawson 1997; Dawson et al. 2008; NIAAA maturing-out reviews.

Cannabis

8 IQ points
average decline from 13 to 38 in persistent users who started in adolescence (Dunedin study)

The Dunedin study followed 1,037 New Zealanders from birth. The decline was concentrated in persistent users who started as teenagers, and quitting did not fully restore function for them.

The challengeRogeberg (2013) showed socioeconomic confounding could reproduce the result, and the true effect could be zero.
The replyThe Dunedin team re-ran it on middle-class children only. The decline was unchanged.

Two less contested findings: daily use of high-potency cannabis carried about 4.8 times the odds of a first psychotic episode in a study across 11 European sites. And people who keep using into adulthood miss the normal rise in conscientiousness.

Meier et al., PNAS 2012; Rogeberg, PNAS 2013; Di Forti et al., Lancet Psychiatry 2019; Littlefield, Sher and Wood 2010.

Facts on risk only. If use is getting hard to control, a doctor or a local service is the right next step.

      Faith and beliefs

      Coming in v2: when people leave or join a religion, and what moves core beliefs after 30.

      Most Americans who leave their childhood faith do so before 24. Most join their current religion before 36. Very few switch after 50.

      Pew Research Center, Faith in Flux, 2009.
