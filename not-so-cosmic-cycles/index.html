Different things lock in at different ages

You may have heard that "your brain finishes at 25". No single study shows that. Instead, each part of life has its own window: a stretch of years when it is easy to shape, after which it gets harder to change. The line on the chart is you.

Why politics swings like a pendulum (maybe)

The idea: each generation picks up its politics from what it sees work or fail when it is about 14 to 24. If one side runs the country badly, the teenagers watching lean the other way for life. A generation later they are the voters, and the pendulum swings back.

This is a claim to test, not a fact. Evidence on both sides at the bottom.

Evidence for

  • The model works. Presidential popularity plus the age curve explains 81% of the ups and downs in US presidential voting over half a century.
  • Generations stay different. White voters born in 1952 are consistently 5 to 10 points more likely to vote Democratic than those born in 1968.
  • The 1972 gap. White first-time voters under 25 backed Nixon at 53%. Those 25 and over: 70%. The biggest age gap in the data.
  • Young minds move most. Studies of the same people over years find views change easily in the late teens and early 20s, then much less.
  • People push back on whoever is in charge. In many countries, when the government spends more, the public starts asking for less, and the reverse.
  • Not just the US. In the UK, people who came of age in decades when the Conservatives dominated lean more Conservative.

Evidence against, or limits

  • Success counts, not only failure. The study itself says generations form over long runs of popular presidents, not one big failure.
  • Adults change too. When the white US South switched from Democrats to Republicans, every age group shifted, not only the young.
  • Labels move more than opinions. Young people in the Reagan years changed their party and what they called themselves, but not most of their views on specific policies.
  • Mostly about white voters. The generation effect is over twice as strong for white Americans as for minorities as a whole.
  • Other splits are bigger. Race, region and class often divide voters more than age does.
  • Hard to prove. Age, year and birth year move together, so any model like this needs assumptions. US data only.

What you live through counts more than what you read about. If markets crash or jobs vanish when you are young, it changes how you think about money and society for life.

Your place settles, but everyone keeps changing

Think of a running race. Your place in the race (your compared with people your age) gets more fixed over time. But the whole race keeps moving: most people get calmer and more organised as they get older.

Can you be kind and still stand your ground? Yes. Caring about people (part of agreeableness) and (part of extraversion) are separate. The trait that clashes with standing firm is politeness, the part of agreeableness that defers to others.

Genes or environment?

How much of the differences between people come from genes ().

Over 100,000 people (Vukasović and Bratko, 2015). Twin studies give 47%, family and adoption studies 22%. The 60% is mostly not your family home: it is your own friends, work, choices and luck.

Can you change on purpose?

Across 207 studies, programmes like therapy shifted trait scores in about 24 weeks, and the change lasted.

The average moved about a third of the normal spread between people (Roberts et al., 2017). Calmness changed most, then extraversion. People who set and actually completed small behaviour goals also moved toward the trait they wanted.

For drugs and alcohol, the age you start matters most

These windows close well before 25. The studies mostly measure when people start and what that predicts later. The status next to each title uses your birth year.

Nicotine

Adults who smoke every day, by when they first tried a cigarette. CDC. In one study of 1,382 smokers, those who started before 20 were more hooked and less likely to quit.

Alcohol

US survey of 27,616 drinkers (Grant and Dawson, 1997). Part of the gap comes from other things, like family history: a 2008 follow-up that controlled for more of them still found about 50% higher odds for a first drink before 15 than at 18 or later.

Many heavy drinkers cut down in their twenties. Moving out of home tends to push drinking up. Marriage, children and work bring it down, most of all for the heaviest drinkers.

Cannabis

A study followed 1,037 New Zealanders from birth to 38. People who used cannabis heavily for years, starting as teenagers, lost about 8 points. Those who started as adults did not show the same drop, and quitting did not fully bring it back for the teen starters.

The challengeAnother researcher showed that family background could create the same result, so the true effect might be zero ().
The replyThe original team re-ran the numbers on middle-class kids only. The drop was the same.

Odds of a first episode, compared with never using

Religion: most changes happen before 24

About 35% of American adults now follow a different religion (or none) than the one they were raised in. Most of the switching happens young, and once a generation has drifted, it does not drift back.
